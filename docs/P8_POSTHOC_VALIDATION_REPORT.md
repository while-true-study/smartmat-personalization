> **Status: post hoc and supplementary.**

>   reviewer-style inspection of the manuscript.
> - They were fully specified in `docs/P8_POSTHOC_VALIDATION_PLAN.md` and

>   files is recorded in every run, table and the provenance (`paper/tables/p8_posthoc_provenance.json`); neither
>   file changed after the results.
> - No v1.0 primary result is replaced. Every result is reported below, including those that contradict the
>   manuscript draft.
> - All tables in this report are generated by `scripts/export_p8_posthoc_tables.py` from

## 1. Motivation

A reviewer reading the draft could offer three simple alternative explanations that the frozen analyses did not
rule out:
1. **Calibration.** The fine-tuning gains might be nothing more than the correction of a domain-level output offset,
   which a constant shift of the base model, or even a constant prediction, could achieve without any
   pressure-dependent learning.
2. **Residual variation.** The pressure model might track little of the within-subject temperature and humidity
   variation beyond a constant level.
3. **Initialization.** The value of the cross-subject pretrained model over training only on the target user's early
   nights was not tested.

## 2. Design (summary of the plan)

- **Frozen inputs, read only:** the P3 checkpoints, training-mean runs and predictions; the P5 runs and plan; the
  v1.0 split files and canonical_v1.
- **Windows:** the RQ2 windows. Subjects User01, User02 and User07 (User02's two mats form one subject);
  temperature and humidity; budgets b = 0, 1, 3, 7, 14; adaptation on the earliest b nights; buffer night b + 1;
  primary span of nights ≥ 16.
- **Predictors:**
  - A: the training mean;
  - B: A + c_b, which equals the adaptation-target mean;
  - C: the RAW-TCN base;
  - D: C + c_b;
  - E: the frozen full fine-tuning.
  - The offset c_b is the mean over the labelled adaptation windows of (y − f(x)). It involves no optimisation, no
    test label, no scaler refit and no hyperparameter.
  - For User02 the offset is pooled over both mats (primary). A per-mat offset is a diagnostic only.
- **Residual variation:**
  - MAE, RMSE and bias;
  - target SD and error SD, both population SDs (ddof = 0, the convention of the existing error decomposition);
  - R = error SD / target SD, a descriptive ratio, not explained variance.
- **Initialization control S (b = 14):**
  - the fold's RAW-TCN architecture, randomly initialised;
  - trained on nights 1–14 with the frozen fine-tuning recipe (only the initialisation differs from E);
  - night 15 unused, evaluation on nights ≥ 16, seeds 0–2.
  - It is an initialization control, not an upper bound on within-subject learnability.
- **Uncertainty:** the frozen P6 night-cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples, RNG seed 0, 95 %). Seed 0 is primary and
  seeds 1–2 are sensitivity. One set of resampled nights per subject is shared by all comparisons.
- **Case map:** the pre-registered interpretation map (plan §8), unchanged.

## 3. Integrity checks (all passed)

- **Base inference:** the frozen P3 base checkpoints reproduced the P5 b = 0 predictions bitwise, for every subject
  and seed.
- **Frozen metrics:**
  - the C (b = 0) and E metrics equal the frozen P5 primary metrics, with a maximum absolute difference of 0;
  - this module's C-vs-E bootstrap equals the frozen P6 ΔMAE table (point and interval), with a maximum absolute
    difference of 0.
- **Identities:**
  - training mean + c_b equals the adaptation-target mean to floating-point precision (≤ 1e-9 is required);
  - constant predictors have R = 1;
  - a constant offset leaves the error SD unchanged.
- **Gates and addendum checks:**
  - every calibration budget and every control run passed the v1.0 leakage gate;
  - the offset and control training windows all lie in the adaptation partition, precede the buffer and test rows,
    and never touch the buffer night or the primary span;
  - the control runs also passed the ten P5 checks, and their initial weights differ from every pretrained base.
- **Coverage:** no per-mat offset was not estimable; both User02 mats have adaptation windows at every budget.
- **Clean rerun:** the whole addendum was rerun into a separate output root (fresh control runs, fresh analysis).
  Result: `scripts/verify_p8_posthoc_reproduction.py`, §9.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_p8_posthoc.py` covers the required rules on synthetic data.

## 6. Does the manuscript story survive?

**Partly. The primary v1.0 results stand unchanged, but their interpretation must be rewritten.**

**What survives:**
- **Offsets dominate the unseen-domain error.** This is strengthened: the offsets are what the adaptation-target
  mean corrects.
- **Negative transfer** is real for User07 temperature at every budget and for User01 humidity at b ≤ 7, and a pure
  offset calibration shows it too.
- **Temporal level mismatch** is the stronger descriptive explanation of negative transfer. It is retrospective.
- **Subject-level reporting** with night-level uncertainty is necessary.

**What does not survive:**
- **Neural personalization as the source of offset correction.** Any reading that credits the corrections to neural
  personalization is not supported. The largest correction (User02 temperature) is achieved by a constant computed
  from the same adaptation labels, with no pressure input.
- **Pressure-based estimation of the microclimate.** Any reading that the pressure models estimate the microclimate
  within a subject is not supported. No pressure-based model reduced the residual variation clearly below that of a
  constant (see the R ranges in the §4 summary), and the base model and the strict-LOSO model had R > 1.
- **The value of cross-subject pretraining.** The pretrained initialisation adds little over within-subject training
  of the same architecture under the same recipe.

**Plain statements required by the task:**
- **A simple constant predictor beats the neural approaches in several settings:**
  - the training mean in strict LOSO for every temperature cell and for User07 humidity;
  - the training mean on the primary span for User07 temperature at every budget;
  - the personalized constant (adaptation-target mean) for User02 temperature at every budget and for User01
    temperature at b ≤ 7.
- **Pressure input adds no demonstrable within-subject predictive value under this protocol.** Where neural models
  beat the personalized constant, their advantage is a level difference on the later span. Whether that difference
  reflects a pressure–microclimate relation or an incidental association cannot be determined with three subjects.

**Title:** re-evaluated in `docs/P8_TITLE_CANDIDATES.md` §6. The evaluation-focused title is recommended (D-058).

## 7. Limitations of the addendum

- **Post hoc.** The analyses were designed after the primary results were known. Their comparators and the case map
  were fixed before they were computed, but the choice to run them was motivated by the results.
- **Cohort.** Three subjects with confounded periods, seasons and mats. The intervals describe within-subject
  night-level uncertainty only, and nights are resampled independently.
- **Initialization control.** The control uses the frozen short fine-tuning schedule. A longer or tuned schedule could
  change the scratch result, so it is not an upper bound. Only one architecture (the fold's source-selected RAW-TCN)
  and only b = 14 were run.
- **Residual ratio.** R is a descriptive ratio of population SDs on a span with drifting levels. It is not explained
  variance and not a significance test. R ≈ 1 does not exclude a small tracking component hidden by noise.
- **Per-mat diagnostic.** Supplementary. It was added after the primary results were known and changes no primary
  comparison.
- **Heater.** Heater and controller state is excluded from the inputs (L9) and remains an unobserved contextual
  factor. No causal statement is made.
