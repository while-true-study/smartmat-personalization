## 1. Question

> Do the neural predictions contain useful temporal co-variation with the target that is hidden by offset and/or
> scale mismatch?

v1.1 showed R ≈ 1 or above. By R² = 1 + Q² − 2rQ, that alone does not exclude a positive correlation r. This
diagnostic measures:
- Q, the prediction scale;
- r_pooled, the correlation over all windows;
- r_within, the correlation of night-centred values, the primary tracking diagnostic;
- r_within_mat, a night × mat-centred sensitivity variant, which matters for User02's two mats;
- the retrospective oracle affine ratio.

All are computed on the existing primary-span predictions; nothing was retrained.

## 2. Integrity checks (all passed)

- **Identity:** R² = 1 + Q² − 2·r_pooled·Q holds in every non-constant row to floating-point precision.
- **D versus C:** D (base + adaptation offset) has exactly C's R, Q and correlations.
- **Constants:** constant predictors (A, B) have R = 1, Q = 0 and all correlations NA.
- **Reconstructed predictions:** they reproduce the frozen v1.1 MAE, RMSE and bias of A–E.
- **Bootstrap:** statistics computed from the night moments equal the window-level statistics.

## 4. Reading (under the pre-registered map)

- **Base model (C).**
  - **Variation without co-variation:** the unadapted network varies substantially (Q well above zero; for User01
    temperature its predictions vary more than the target), but it shows little linear co-variation with the target
    within nights for any subject (J1, or J2 for User07 temperature, where a small pooled association has no
    within-night counterpart).
  - **User02, negative correlations (J4):** both the pooled and the night-centred correlations are negative, while
    the night × mat-centred correlation is small. Most of the negative association comes from the between-mat level
    difference inside a night; the learned variation is misaligned in this domain. No causal inversion is claimed.
- **Full fine-tuning (E).**
  - **Up to seven nights:** the cells are J1 or J4. No within-night co-variation is found.
  - **At b = 14, User01 temperature and both User02 targets:** the pooled correlations turn positive, with R ≈ 1 and
    Q > 0 (J2, J6).
    - User01 temperature: the within-night correlation stays near zero.
    - User02: the night-centred correlation is positive, but the night × mat-centred correlation is near zero.
      After fine-tuning, the model reproduces the two mats' level difference, which is level information, not
      tracking. The pre-registered User02 rule therefore does not count it as within-night co-variation.
  - **Negative pooled correlations (J4):** User07 temperature and User01 humidity at b = 14.
- **Scratch control (S, b = 14).**
  - The pattern is the same as for E.
  - One cell, User02 temperature, meets the within-night rule (J3), with a small night × mat-centred correlation.
    It is a single subject and target, not a consistent pattern.
- **Oracle headroom (J7 almost everywhere).**
  - Even an affine map fitted retrospectively on the test labels, which would be leakage, could lower the residual
    SD only slightly in any cell. The trigger condition (oracle ratio ≤ 0.90 for two subjects on the same target)
    is met nowhere.
  - **The adaptation-only affine comparator was therefore not run (pre-specified trigger not fired).**

## 5. Answer to the question

- **No consistent within-night co-variation.** The neural predictions contain no consistent within-night
  co-variation with the measured temperature or humidity in any model condition. The pre-registered headline
  requires at least two subjects; the best case is one.
- **Pooled associations are level alignments.** Where a pooled association appears (after 14 nights of adaptation),
  it reflects between-night or between-mat level alignment rather than within-night tracking. It is small and does
  not reduce the residual variation below that of a constant.
- **No hidden linear structure.** The retrospective oracle shows that no substantial linear structure is hidden by
  offset or scale mismatch. Affine recalibration could not have turned these predictions into a clearly better
  tracker, even with access to the test labels.
- **Classification (question 11): at most an association between nights (or between mats), and no genuine
  within-night co-variation.** The base model has no useful co-variation.
- **Consequence for the manuscript:** the v1.1 interpretation survives and is sharpened. Under the tested 40-s
  RAW-pressure representation, TCN architecture and fixed training and adaptation schedules, the neural predictions
  showed no consistent advantage over simple level baselines, and no hidden dynamic signal was found that
  calibration could recover. Simple level baselines remain the practical comparator to beat.

## 6. Limitations of this diagnostic

- **Post hoc (second order):** the design was fixed before the computation, but the decision to run it followed the
  v1.1 results.
- **Linear only:** the correlations and the oracle ratio are linear diagnostics. A nonlinear relation, or a relation
  at a longer time scale than the 40-s window, is not excluded.
- **Night-centring** removes between-night trends by design. Slow within-night drifts shared by prediction and target
  still count as within-night co-variation.
- **Uncertainty:** three subjects; the intervals describe within-subject night-level uncertainty only, and nights
  are resampled independently.
- **Thresholds:** the 0.10 correlation floor and the 0.90 oracle trigger are pragmatic thresholds, not significance
  thresholds.
