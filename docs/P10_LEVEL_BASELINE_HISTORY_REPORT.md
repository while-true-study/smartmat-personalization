## 1. Question

> How much of the error reduction after personalization is explained by simple level correction, and does pressure
> input contribute beyond that to tracking variation on the later evaluation span?

P10 has three parts, run in order: R (reproduction check of the frozen mean-baseline and neural metrics), M (median
baselines), H (Ridge and histogram gradient boosting on pressure summaries over 40-s, 300-s and 900-s histories, strict
LOSO).

**Reading.**
- **Strict LOSO.** The median changes which cells favour the constant, but the temperature finding for User02 and
  User07 does not depend on the choice: both constants have a lower MAE than the RAW-TCN there. For User01 temperature
  the median is worse than the RAW-TCN while the mean is better; for User07 humidity both constants beat the RAW-TCN.
  The statement "the network did not beat a training-mean predictor for temperature in any held-out subject" is
  specific to the mean.
- **Personalization.** The adaptation-target median is not above full fine-tuning in fewer cells than the mean
  (Table P10-4). Many "B ≤ E" cells are close calls: the medians of integer-valued targets sit on whole degrees or
  percentage points, and a shift of the level by a fraction of a unit flips the point comparison. The frozen
  night-level intervals of v1.1 already classify B as better than E in fewer cells than the point count, and E as better
  than B in more.
- **Consequence.** "A personalized constant was not worse than full fine-tuning in 12 of 24 cells" is true for the
  mean and must be reported as a point-estimate count that depends on the choice of constant. The substantive reading
  that survives is narrower: where full fine-tuning beat a personalized constant, it did so through a level closer to
  the later span (bias, v1.1), and in several cells a constant computed from the adaptation labels came within the
  seed spread of fine-tuning or below it.

- **H-a, temperature.** Ridge beat the common-pool training mean in no subject. Histogram boosting with a 300-s or

  than the constant. By the pre-registered statement, **the 40-s RAW-TCN negative result does not extend to every
  pressure model for temperature**.
- **H-a, humidity.** Both families beat the common-pool training mean for User01 and User02 at every history and were
  worse for User07. This reproduces the pattern of the frozen 40-s RAW-TCN under strict LOSO (better than the training
  mean for User01 and User02 humidity, worse for User07); it is not new evidence for longer history.
- **How the gains arise.** Every H-a gain comes with a smaller absolute bias than the constant's, and R stays above 1
  in those cells. The night-centred correlations are near zero or negative, with one exception: Ridge for User02

  depends on the mean being the reference constant.
- **H-b, longer history.** For temperature a longer history had a lower error in all three subjects for histogram
  boosting (300 s and 900 s) and for Ridge at 300 s, and in two subjects for Ridge at 900 s; for humidity only boosting
  at 900 s met the rule. By the plan's wording: **longer pressure history was associated with lower temperature error
  for both families**. This is an association on the common subset; it is not evidence of a thermal time constant or
  lag, and it was largely accompanied by a smaller bias (for boosting also by a lower R, which stayed above 1).

## 5. What changes and what stays

- **Stays:**
  - no pressure-based model in P3–P10 demonstrated within-night co-variation with temperature or humidity that goes
    beyond level (v1.2 for the TCNs, H-c here for the summary models);
  - personalization gains were mainly level corrections; a personalized constant is the comparator any adaptive
    estimator must be reported against;
  - User02's post-adaptation night-centred correlations reflect the two mats' level difference (v1.2 night × mat).
- **Changes (scope of the negative result):**
  - "pressure-based estimation showed no consistent advantage over simple level baselines" holds for the 40-s RAW-TCN
    and Ridge, but not for every pressure model: under strict LOSO a histogram-boosting model on 300-s or 900-s pressure
    summaries had a lower temperature error than the source training mean in two of three held-out subjects, on the
    subset of endpoints with 15 min of continuous history, through a smaller level offset rather than tracking;
  - the "12 of 24" count depends on using the mean as the personalized constant; the median gives a smaller count;
  - "the network did not beat the training mean for temperature" is mean-specific for User01.
- **Not supported by P10:** thermal lag, occupancy duration, pressure information "absent", TCN overfitting, or any
  population statement.

## 6. Limitations

- Post hoc, designed with all earlier results known; three subjects; nights resampled independently.
- Part H uses about half of the labelled endpoints (15 min of gap-free history), which may favour stable, long
  recordings; the constants and the RAW-TCN were re-evaluated on the same subset for that reason.
- One tree family with a small fixed grid; some selected values lie at grid edges.
- Histogram boosting is deterministic for a fixed thread count and `random_state` on this machine (clean rerun, §7); a
  different thread count or build may change the last bits.
- The summary features are generic per-channel statistics. Channel geometry is unknown; pressure levels are contact
  proxies and change sums are not counts of movements.
